## What is working well

A few things stand out as strong.

The early part is well judged:

* explain Fabric only in relation to the Day 1 pipeline
* let learners investigate the environment
* then discuss what is the same and what is new

That should help stop the platform from feeling abstract.

The middle is also good:

* demo first
* then learners recreate the flow
* then pause and discuss familiarity vs difference

That is a sensible teaching rhythm.

The end of the day is probably the strongest part:

* you have a K13 lens
* a local vs cloud comparison
* a clear statement that cloud does not automatically mean good architecture
* a bridge to Day 3

That makes the day do real conceptual work, not just technical repetition.

## My main suggestion

The only part I would watch is the jump from:

**14:50 - K13 lens**
to
**15:00 - groups compare**

That is fine, but I would make sure the K13 lens is not just a mini lecture. It will probably work best if it is introduced as the structure for the comparison itself.

So rather than:

* 10 minutes on K13
* then groups compare

I would think:

* briefly introduce the four lenses
* then groups compare using those lenses

In other words, K13 becomes the framework for the breakout, not a separate content block.

## Why that matters

Because then the flow becomes:

* what changed technically?
* what changed operationally?
* what changed in security / scale / governance / cost terms?
* what still is not solved?

That is a much stronger Day 2 reflection.

## A slight refinement

If you wanted to tighten it a little, the last part could conceptually be:

* **14:50** Introduce the comparison lenses: security, scalability, governance, cost
* **15:00** Groups compare Day 1 local vs Day 2 Fabric
* **15:20** Share back
* **15:30** Whole-class synthesis: cloud improves some things, but architecture is still fragile

That would make the ending feel even cleaner.
