Day 4 should feel narrower and calmer than Day 3.

Day 3 is the redesign day.
Day 4 is the **make it usable by other people** day.

So I would frame it as:

## Day 4 - Supportable and Explainable

**Holding question**
What would it take for someone who did not build this pipeline to run it, trust it, and explain it to a stakeholder?

That question gives you a very clean final-day shape.

---

## Suggested Day 4 structure

### 1. Reconnect to Day 3 - 15 to 20 mins

**Focus**
Remind learners what changed yesterday.

**Topics**

* the pipeline still produces the same business output
* what changed was its structure
* today is about making it trustworthy, understandable, and usable beyond the people who built it

**Why this matters**
It makes Day 4 feel like the next logical step rather than a loose ending.

---

### 2. Validation and trust - 35 to 45 mins

**Focus**
How do we know the pipeline is still working?

**Topics**

* what kinds of silent failures can happen
* where checks should sit
* simple validation before data moves from one layer to the next

**Examples**

* row count sanity checks
* null checks on required fields
* duplicate checks
* valid product IDs before join
* non-negative quantities or values

**Activity**
Learners review the current pipeline and decide:

* what should be checked at bronze to silver?
* what should be checked at silver to gold?
* which checks are essential and which are nice to have?

Then add or sketch a few checks in practice.

**Output**
A small set of practical validation checks tied to the layered pipeline.

---

### 3. Documentation and dependencies - 35 to 45 mins

**Focus**
How does someone else understand the pipeline?

**Topics**

* minimum viable documentation
* sources, layers, outputs
* what depends on what
* what another engineer would need to know to orientate themselves

**Activity**
Learners produce a lightweight handover view, for example:

* source -> silver -> gold flow
* what each layer contains
* what runs first
* what the final output is for

This could be:

* a simple table
* a diagram
* a short structured note

**Prompts**

* what would a new engineer need to know first?
* what would confuse them if undocumented?
* what assumptions have we made that are currently only in our heads?

**Output**
A minimum viable pipeline handover artefact.

---

### 4. Stakeholder explanation - 30 to 40 mins

**Focus**
How do we explain the value without drowning people in engineering detail?

**Topics**

* technical view vs stakeholder view
* how to explain what the output is for
* how to explain trust and limitations in plain language

**Activity**
Pairs or small groups prepare a short explanation of:

* what the trusted output is
* what business question it helps answer
* why it can be trusted more than the raw sources
* what caveats still remain

You could frame it as:
“Explain orders_with_detail to a manager who does not care about bronze, silver, or notebooks.”

**Prompts**

* what would they care about?
* what would make them trust it?
* what would make them sceptical?

**Output**
A short business-facing explanation or mini pitch.

---

### 5. Next maturity steps - 20 to 30 mins

**Focus**
Where would the pipeline go next if the team had more time?

**Topics**

* IoT as a richer event source
* Marketing as a richer customer-facing source
* what a more mature data team might add next
* why these were not core to this module

**Activity**
Whole-class discussion or small-group thought exercise:

* what would we add next?
* which source would add the most value?
* which would add the most complexity?
* what would need to change in the architecture?

**Output**
A realistic sense of scope, boundaries, and future development.

---

### 6. Module retrospective and close - 15 to 20 mins

**Focus**
Make the journey visible.

**Prompts**

* what is different now compared with Day 1?
* what would you do differently if you rebuilt the pipeline?
* what have you learned about ETL beyond just moving data?
* where does this sit in the wider programme lifecycle?

**Good closing message**
We started by making data work.
We then moved it into the cloud, structured it better, and made it easier to trust and explain.
That is the middle of the data engineering lifecycle in action.

---

## A possible schedule in your style

### Day 4 - Supportable & Explainable

* reconnect to Day 3 and set the holding question
* validation and trust: what should we check, and where?
* documentation and dependencies: what would another engineer need?
* stakeholder explanation: how do we explain the result simply?
* next maturity steps: IoT and Marketing as realistic extensions
* module retrospective and close

---

## Suggested WebEx use

Day 4 is well suited to:

* **whole-class discussion** for validation ideas
* **pairs or breakout rooms** for documentation and stakeholder explanation
* **report-back** for comparing what different groups thought was most important

I would especially use breakout rooms for:

* minimum viable documentation task
* stakeholder explanation task

Those are both good because they have a clear output and compare well in plenary.

---

## What Day 4 should leave learners with

By the end of Day 4, learners should be able to say:

* I know what checks make this pipeline more trustworthy
* I can explain the flow and dependencies to another engineer
* I can explain the business value of the output to a stakeholder
* I can see how the pipeline could mature beyond this module
* I understand how this module fits into the wider lifecycle

---

## The main thing to avoid

Do not let Day 4 become:

* a grab-bag of leftover topics
  or
* a mini Module 6

It should stay focused on:
**trust, handover, explanation, and next-step maturity**
