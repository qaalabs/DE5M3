setup -> context -> inspect raw data -> identify issues -> clean -> flatten and join -> use the result -> reflect.

That is a strong Day 1 arc.

A few thoughts.

The **best part** is that the day has a visible finish line:
they do not just clean data, they end with a trusted dataset and a simple business question. That gives the practical work purpose.

I also like that you have placed the **discussion after touching the data** rather than before. That usually makes the conversation much better because learners have something concrete to say.

What I would watch is the section from **11:00 to lunch**. At the moment it is quite inspect-and-discuss heavy:

* inspect Sales
* inspect Product
* discuss problems
* overview of cleaning moves

That is sensible, but it risks feeling a little static unless learners actually do a tiny first transformation before lunch. Even one small move could help - for example converting a field, spotting a duplicate, or fixing one obvious issue in Sales. That would make the morning feel more like “we started the ETL” rather than “we studied the sources”.

The afternoon looks stronger because it has more momentum:

* clean
* discuss judgement
* flatten and join
* answer a question
* reflect

That is probably where the day will feel most satisfying.

I would also think about **VM Setup at 9:40**. If that is unavoidable, fine, but it can eat the energy of the opening. If possible, I would keep tying it back to the day’s purpose so it does not feel like admin before the real session starts.

So my main suggestion is:

**keep this structure, but consider making the morning end with one tiny hands-on cleaning win before lunch.**

That would make the whole day feel even more like:
inspect -> start fixing -> continue fixing -> combine -> use.

As a Day 1 plan, though, this is good. It feels coherent and teachable.
