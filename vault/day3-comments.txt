This looks strong.

It reads like a proper Day 3 and, more importantly, it feels different from Day 1 and Day 2 in the right way. It is less about “doing the next lab” and more about **reviewing, redesigning, and justifying**.

A few things are working very well.

The opening is good because you are not starting with architecture vocabulary. You are starting with **the Day 2 pipeline as it stands**. That keeps the whole day grounded.

The sequence from:

* show the pipeline
* surface weaknesses
* build a list of design debts
* then introduce bronze/silver/gold

is exactly the right pedagogical move. It means the architecture is an answer, not a lecture topic.

The afternoon is also well judged:

* refactor first
* then pause to interpret what changed
* then apply the same thinking to an extension source

That gives the day both a shared core and group variation.

## What I especially like

This line is doing a lot of good work:

**Pipeline still answers the same question - design is clearer**

That is the essence of Day 3.

## My only suggestions

They are small.

### 1. 11:30 “practice” might really be more “guided design”

At 11:30, “Map the Day 2 pipeline onto B/S/G” is slightly more of a design/thinking task than hands-on practice.

That is not a problem, but in your own notes you might think of it as:

* guided mapping
  or
* guided design review

rather than practice in the same sense as notebook work.

### 2. 13:20 is the key session of the day

That is the session I would protect most carefully.
It is where the architecture becomes real.

So when you build the actual materials later, that is the one that will need the clearest scaffold:

* what already exists from Day 2
* what learners are changing
* what “explicit layers” should look like
* what the lightweight checks are

### 3. 15:00 group design task is very good for WebEx

That is probably the best breakout room use of the day.
It is contained, purposeful, and gives a good report-back.

You may want to make sure each group has a very simple shared template, something like:

* bronze contains...
* silver contains...
* key transformations...
* validation checks...
* feeds which gold output...
* awkward part of this source...

That will make the report-back cleaner.

## My overall verdict

Yes - this is a good Day 3 outline.

It feels:

* coherent
* intellectually richer
* clearly connected to Day 2
* properly positioned as the “value-add” day
* well suited to virtual delivery with a mix of whole-class and breakout work

So I would say Day 3 is now in a good enough state to move on to Day 4 rather than keep reworking it.
